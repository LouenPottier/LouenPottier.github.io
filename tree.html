Six-degree-of-freedom test case: a small pine tree pushed by hand

A potted pine tree, filmed with the built-in webcam of a low-end laptop (30 takes of a few seconds, the tree pushed by hand then left to swing freely, one take held out). No GPU was used anywhere in this experiment. The encoder is not a network here: 595 keypoints are tracked in every take and a single SVD of their displacements gives a latent state q: twelve coordinates were kept for training, then the six that do not oscillate (slow drifts, invisible on screen) were removed by marginalising the Gaussians, leaving a 6-D state whose first two coordinates are the two sway directions of the trunk and the others the motion of individual branches. The dynamics is the simplest possible Lagrangian, a linear oscillator q̈ = −K q − C q̇ + b with 6×6 stiffness and damping matrices (all six modes oscillate, between 2.7 and 4.8 Hz), fitted on the rollouts of the 29 training takes. The Gaussians are parameterised by (x, y, q₁, …, q₆) with a full 8×8 covariance each and were trained on a CPU with a small local-window rasteriser. On the held-out take, decoding the rollout of the oscillator reproduces the filmed video at 32.9 dB, against 33.1 dB when decoding the measured state.

grab & drag a branch
⟶ Force f applied by dragging, pulled back to the 6-D latent space through Jᵀf, J being the Jacobian of the grabbed gaussians. Local mode: the gaussians under the cursor, weighted by their footprint. Global mode: every mobile gaussian inside the disc weighs the same, which excites the whole-tree modes rather than one branch. Tinted area: pixels that never moved in the 30 takes; with the freeze toggle on, gaussians centred there are held at their rest state instead of following q.
