LaGSplat: a small pine tree, filmed with a webcam

Unlisted test case·2026·Main LaGSplat page·arXiv:2608.16324·Code

A potted pine tree, filmed with the built-in webcam of a low-end laptop (30 takes of a few seconds, the tree pushed by hand then left to swing freely, one take held out). No GPU was used anywhere in this experiment. The encoder is not a network here: 595 keypoints are tracked in every take and a single SVD of their displacements gives a latent state q: twelve coordinates were kept for training, then the six that do not oscillate (slow drifts, invisible on screen) were removed by marginalising the Gaussians, leaving a 6-D state whose first two coordinates are the two sway directions of the trunk and the others the motion of individual branches. The dynamics is the simplest possible Lagrangian, a linear oscillator q̈ = −K q − C q̇ + b with 6×6 stiffness and damping matrices (all six modes oscillate, between 2.7 and 4.8 Hz), fitted on the rollouts of the 29 training takes. The Gaussians are parameterised by (x, y, q₁, …, q₆) with a full 8×8 covariance each and were trained on a CPU with a small local-window rasteriser.
